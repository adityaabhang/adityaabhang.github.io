Overview

At Purdue's IDEAS Lab, I'm developing a fully autonomous drone system capable of navigating through dense forest canopies while tracking human subjects in real-time. The project addresses a critical gap in search-and-rescue and environmental monitoring: most existing drone systems fail in heavily occluded, GPS-denied environments where tree cover disrupts both navigation and target visibility.

The system pairs a human detection, tracking, and trajectory-prediction pipeline with a safety-assured LiDAR planner, joined by a bridge I built that turns "where the person is going" into flight goals without ever letting the follower override obstacle avoidance. A multi-agent communication layer relays live target coordinates from the scout to a parent UAS so coverage continues when the scout loses line-of-sight.

Methodology

The pipeline is built around three core subsystems that work in concert:

1. Perception & Obstacle Avoidance: LiDAR-inertial odometry (FAST-LIO2) provides a light-independent state estimate, and an incrementally-inflated occupancy grid (ROG-Map) turns each LiDAR scan into the free/occupied volume the planner reasons over. Planning and collision avoidance are handled by the SUPER planner (HKU MaRS, Science Robotics 2025), used unmodified: it builds IRIS/CIRI safe flight corridors through the canopy, optimises a MINCO trajectory inside those hard constraints, replans at 15 Hz against the live map, and falls back to an emergency-stop trajectory if a replan fails.

2. Human Tracking & Trajectory Prediction: A YOLO detector (TensorRT on the onboard GPU) feeds BoT-SORT multi-object tracking with camera-motion compensation and ReID, so an identity survives the multi-second full occlusions that happen every time a subject passes behind a trunk. An EigenTrajectory model predicts each person's next ~1.5 s as a 20-point path, which is what lets the system keep moving toward a target it currently cannot see.

3. Bridge to the Planner: I built a ROS 2 package, dtp_super_bridge, that converts the tracker/predictor output into a single goal pose for SUPER — the programmatic equivalent of a map click. The bridge never plans a path; SUPER keeps full authority over obstacle avoidance. A four-state behaviour machine decides the goal: FOLLOW (hold a 2 m standoff behind the predicted position, placed opposite the person's travel direction so it can't run away on overshoot), HOLD & EXTRAPOLATE then HOLD POSITION (advance the goal along the last heading for 1.2 s when the track drops, then freeze and hover), and BACK OFF (retreat when the person comes within 1.5 m, with hysteresis on exit). Priority is BACK OFF > HOLD > FOLLOW, and safety transitions bypass the goal-jitter deadband.

The upstream detector/predictor and the planner each still run standalone; every repo-crossing name, remap, and coordinate transform lives in the one bridge node.

Challenges

Dense occlusion handling: Forest environments create persistent, unpredictable occlusions. A target can be fully visible one frame and completely hidden the next as they move behind a tree trunk. Standard trackers lose identity quickly in these conditions. We addressed this by integrating ReID features that maintain identity across multi-second occlusion gaps, and by using trajectory prediction to estimate target position during blind periods.

Variable lighting conditions: Canopy-filtered sunlight creates extreme dynamic range — bright patches of direct light next to deep shadow. This wreaks havoc on both detection confidence and appearance-based ReID. We implemented adaptive exposure management and lighting-invariant feature representations to maintain tracking consistency.

Keeping obstacle avoidance above target-following: A follower that chases a noisy position estimate will eventually chase it into a tree. The architecture makes avoidance structurally dominant rather than relying on tuning — the bridge only ever proposes a goal, and the planner projects every goal onto the nearest free cell of the live occupancy map, rejects goals that are deeply inside an obstacle, and brakes if it cannot find a safe trajectory.

Real-time constraint on embedded hardware: The whole loop has to run on drone-mounted compute with a fixed latency budget. Teammate Ayush Kumar runs a parallel YOLO + BoT-SORT + EigenTrajectory pipeline on a Jetson; we benchmark throughput against accuracy to pick the operating point for flight.
